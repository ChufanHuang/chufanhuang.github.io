Chufan Huang

Responsible AI and computational social science — GenAI evaluation, misinformation, human–AI interaction, and visual media.

I’m a master’s student in Internet & Society at the Institute of Communication and Media Research (IKMZ), University of Zurich, focusing on computational communication, responsible AI, misinformation, and human–AI interaction. I have also completed graduate coursework at ETH Zurich in social network modeling and digital social dynamics.

My research examines how information, credibility, and cultural meaning are produced and reshaped in platform-mediated environments. One line of work studies the long-term evolution of science-related conspiracy narratives on social media, with attention to temporal visibility, framing structures, and discursive positioning. A second line of work examines generative AI systems as sociotechnical objects, especially how language, prompts, training data, and model design shape visual representation, cultural specificity, and model-specific biases.

Methodologically, I work end-to-end with digital traces and AI-generated outputs. I use Python/R workflows for data cleaning, temporal aggregation, NLP-based text analysis, topic modeling, and sentiment analysis. I also apply multimodal evaluation methods, including CLIP-based image-text similarity, UMAP projections, qualitative visual analysis, and reverse image search, to audit AI-generated images and study multilingual prompting, cultural representation, and aesthetic flattening.

Before Zurich, I earned an M.A. in Journalism & Communication at Sichuan International Studies University, where I developed a theoretical and qualitative foundation in mediatization, platform studies, discourse analysis, ethnography, interviews, and grounded-theory-inspired research.

In short, my first master’s emphasized theory and qualitative interpretation; my current training extends those questions through computational, multimodal, and evaluation-oriented methods. I am particularly interested in how AI systems retrieve, generate, represent, and communicate information in ways that are reliable, transparent, and socially responsible.
